1. Corporate architecture and regulatory domicile

Project Delper is a private global digital infrastructure ecosystem with a dual-entity architecture centered in Singapore. Active commercial operations (software engineering, ad platform management and data telemetry clearing) sit inside a Singapore Holding Company (HoldCo) parent structure.

To comply with the Accounting and Corporate Regulatory Authority (ACRA) and the Monetary Authority of Singapore (MAS), investments are separated into an onshore Singapore Variable Capital Company (VCC). The VCC is a passive fund that manages fixed-income assets and real estate equities under its licensed fund manager.

The ecosystem is anchored by an initial, non-leveraged growth equity injection of $300 million from a Single Family Office (SFO). To maintain data confidentiality, the whole corporate footprint is chartered to remain permanently private.

4. Global onboarding and strict verification architecture

User acquisition integrates with existing regional digital payment architectures, mobile data infrastructure and non-invasive software endpoints. To align with global privacy frameworks (including GDPR in Europe and state laws such as Illinois BIPA in the United States), no biometric details are collected, stored or processed anywhere in the network.

  • Mandatory identification protocol. To verify unique identity and authorize redemption of closed-loop rewards, every user completes a streamlined digital Know Your Customer (KYC) check tied to local structural registries.

In highly restrictive privacy jurisdictions, the interface runs on a zero-biometric haptic fallback to avoid camera tracking while still verifying human command.

5. Sovereign capital reinvestment and global GDP expansion model

A structured fixed-income rollover engine matches real-world market capacity, capital safety and sovereign yield structures.

Years 1–28: capital accumulation

The passive Singapore VCC deploys $180.408 billion a year (the 30% allocation) into senior-secured, 20-year Sovereign Government Infrastructure Bonds, benchmarked to emerging-market public infrastructure yields. Projects (transit, utilities, logistics corridors) are executed by third-party global EPC contractors under a BOOT (Build-Own-Operate-Transfer) framework.

Year 29 onwards: continuous roll-over

As 20-year tranches mature, principal returns to the VCC treasury and the physical assets revert 100% to host nations. This removes long-term maintenance and depreciation liabilities from Delper's ledger, so the investment manager can reinvest the principal into fresh sovereign bond tranches.

Direct macroeconomic impact

By anchoring these multi-billion dollar annual purchases into specific-performance infrastructure tranches, the VCC's bond purchases stimulate global economic output. The injection into hard public assets is intended to trigger a fiscal multiplier, lifting global GDP through raw materials procurement, industrial contract velocity and supply-side logistics efficiency.

6. Institutional guardrails and risk shielding

  • Financial shielding. The platform operates as an asset-light data clearinghouse with no direct loan underwriting, default or credit risk. 100% of consumer credit exposure passes to partner institutional banking networks.
  • Technology sandboxing. Computation runs on hardcoded hardware infrastructure protected by one-way entry registries and secure front-end components. AI tools act purely as data porters with zero executive command authority, backed by automated sandboxed filters on custom front-end chips to prevent logic drift.

7. Viability, financial ratios and economic moat

  • Breakeven profile. A Day 1 operating surplus: core software subscriptions and media indexing fees are invoiced and collected upfront before scaling, so HoldCo keeps a near-zero capital burn rate.
  • Debt service coverage ratio. Infinite, because the capital layout is entirely equity-funded and non-leveraged.
  • Core operating margin. Above 95% across all SaaS and clearing lines, by passing physical inventory, storage and logistics liabilities to third-party suppliers through automated SLAs.
  • Tax optimization. Local indirect and municipal taxes are paid at source in host jurisdictions. Post-tax residual software profits route to the Singapore HoldCo and parent entities, where foreign-sourced dividends and bond coupons clear under the Section 13U Scheme and Singapore's single-tier framework.
  • The closed-loop moat. Returning 30% of operating revenue to users as closed-loop store credits keeps transaction velocity inside the Delper partner merchant framework, legally binding enterprise vendors to HoldCo's predictive intent data network.
